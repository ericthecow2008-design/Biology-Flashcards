**Q1. [보기 Combination · 3점] A warning label on diet soda**

Phenylketonuria (PKU) is an inherited disease in which the enzyme phenylalanine hydroxylase doesn't work; newborns are screened for it with a heel-prick blood test. Using the diagram, which statements in <보기> are correct?

**◇ Diagram — Where phenylalanine goes.** Pathway diagram: phenylalanine from food protein is converted by phenylalanine hydroxylase to tyrosine, which leads on to melanin and to dopamine and adrenaline. A dashed minor side route leads from phenylalanine to phenylpyruvate, a phenylketone.

> **(가)** Untreated, phenylalanine builds up in the blood to levels that harm the developing brain.
>
> **(나)** Drinks sweetened with aspartame carry the warning "Phenylketonurics: contains phenylalanine".

**<보기>**
- ㄱ. Untreated children with PKU often have lighter skin and hair than their relatives, since less tyrosine is made for the route to melanin.
- ㄴ. Since the side route's enzyme works normally in PKU, phenylpyruvate is made at its usual low rate even as phenylalanine builds up as in (가).
- ㄷ. The warning in (나) matters because the phenylalanine released when aspartame is digested enters the same pathway as phenylalanine from dietary protein.

① ㄷ　 ② ㄱ, ㄴ　 ③ ㄱ, ㄷ　 ④ ㄴ, ㄷ　 ⑤ ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ

**Answer: ③ ㄱ, ㄷ**
- ㄱ — **O** — Linked pathways mean a failed step cuts the supply to everything after it: less tyrosine is made from phenylalanine for the melanin route, and the excess phenylalanine also blocks tyrosinase, the first enzyme on that route, so less pigment is made. (PKU medical formulas are supplemented with tyrosine for this reason.)
- ㄴ — **X** — That enzyme is unchanged, but far more phenylalanine reaches it, so far more phenylpyruvate is made; these phenylketones spill into the urine and give PKU its name.
- ㄷ — **O** — Aspartame is built from aspartate and phenylalanine; digesting it frees phenylalanine, which adds to the load the blocked pathway can't clear.

*Concepts linked:* Metabolic pathways are linked: a product of one step is the next step's reactant · Real-world: phenylketonuria (PKU)

### ATP & Energetic Coupling

**Q3. [보기 Combination · 3점] Creatine in a sprinter's muscle**

Creatine is one of the most popular sports supplements and modestly improves performance in repeated short sprints. Using the diagram, which statements in <보기> are correct?

**◇ Diagram — Energy released by removing a phosphate.** Horizontal bar chart of the free-energy change when one phosphate is hydrolyzed off three molecules: phosphocreatine −10.3, ATP to ADP −7.3, glucose 6-phosphate −3.3 kcal/mol.

> **(가)** In the first seconds of intense exercise, phosphocreatine passes its phosphate directly to ADP, making ATP and creatine.
>
> **(나)** Each value in the diagram is for removing one phosphate from that molecule.

**<보기>**
- ㄱ. Creatine supplements help because muscle cells oxidize the extra creatine itself for energy, in the same way that they oxidize glucose and fatty acids.
- ㄴ. Under standard conditions the transfer in (가) has a ΔG of about −3.0 kcal/mol, so it runs forward without any other energy source.
- ㄷ. The reaction creatine + ATP → phosphocreatine + ADP can't run forward in muscle, since its standard ΔG is positive.

① ㄴ　 ② ㄱ, ㄴ　 ③ ㄱ, ㄷ　 ④ ㄴ, ㄷ　 ⑤ ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ

**Answer: ① ㄴ**
- ㄱ — **X** — Creatine is a phosphate carrier, not a fuel: at rest it is recharged to phosphocreatine, and during effort phosphocreatine hands the phosphate to ADP. Extra creatine means a bigger phosphocreatine store for remaking ATP quickly.
- ㄴ — **O** — Coupling phosphocreatine hydrolysis (−10.3) with ATP synthesis from ADP + Pi (+7.3) gives −10.3 + 7.3 = −3.0 kcal/mol.
- ㄷ — **X** — Its standard ΔG is indeed +10.3 − 7.3 = +3.0 kcal/mol, but resting muscle keeps ATP high and ADP very low, and creatine is plentiful after exercise; with reactants held above their equilibrium ratio to products, the actual ΔG is negative, so phosphocreatine is rebuilt.

*Concepts linked:* Energetic coupling through a shared phosphate · ATP hydrolysis is exergonic · Real-world: creatine supplements

### Thermodynamics & Free Energy

**Q4. [보기 Combination · 3점] Click, and the pack gets hot**

A reusable hand warmer holds a sodium acetate solution that stays liquid at room temperature even though it holds more dissolved salt than would normally stay dissolved. Using the diagram, which statements in <보기> are correct?

**◇ Diagram — Free energy of a hand-warmer pack.** Free-energy profile of a reusable hand warmer: a supersaturated sodium acetate solution sits on a higher plateau, a hump rises between it and a lower plateau labelled sodium acetate crystals.

> **(가)** Clicking a small metal disc inside the pack starts crystals forming, and the pack warms to about 50-55 °C as it turns solid.
>
> **(나)** To reuse the pack, it is boiled in water until the crystals dissolve, then left to cool without crystallizing.

**<보기>**
- ㄱ. Clicking the disc supplies the energy that makes the crystallization's ΔG negative, which is why the solution stays liquid until the disc is clicked.
- ㄴ. Crystallizing makes the sodium acetate more ordered, so it is spontaneous only because the heat it releases (negative ΔH) outweighs the drop in entropy.
- ㄷ. Resetting the pack in (나) takes an energy input, since going from crystals back to the room-temperature solution has a positive ΔG.

① ㄴ　 ② ㄷ　 ③ ㄱ, ㄴ　 ④ ㄴ, ㄷ　 ⑤ ㄱ, ㄴ, ㄷ

**Answer: ④ ㄴ, ㄷ**
- ㄱ — **X** — ΔG is set by the gap between the two plateaus, which the click doesn't change. The click only gets the first crystals past the activation-energy barrier; after that, crystallization carries on by itself.
- ㄴ — **O** — In ΔG = ΔH − TΔS, a negative ΔS makes −TΔS positive; ΔG is still negative because ΔH is more negative.
- ㄷ — **O** — At room temperature, crystals → solution is the reverse of an exergonic change, so ΔG is positive. Boiling works because at high T the −TΔS term makes dissolving spontaneous; careful cooling then leaves the solution on the higher plateau.

*Concepts linked:* ΔG = ΔH − TΔS · Activation energy · Exergonic vs endergonic · Real-world: reusable hand warmers

**Q5. [Multiple Choice · 2점]**

Refrigerating food slows its spoilage, which depends on enzyme-catalyzed reactions in microbes and in the food itself. Which explanation fits best?

① The cold lowers the free energy each reaction releases until it reaches zero　 ② The cold makes the spoilage reactions endergonic, so they can no longer proceed　 ③ The cold raises the activation energy that each spoilage reaction needs　 ④ The cold unfolds the enzymes, so their active sites can no longer bind substrates　 ⑤ The cold leaves fewer molecules energetic enough to cross the activation barrier

**Answer: ⑤ The cold leaves fewer molecules energetic enough to cross the activation barrier**

Reactants must have enough kinetic energy to reach the transition state; cooling slows molecules, so fewer succeed per second. ΔG shifts only slightly and stays negative, the barrier's height is unchanged, and fridge temperatures don't denature enzymes.

*Concepts linked:* Activation energy · Kinetic energy · Real-world: refrigeration

**Q8. [True / False · 2점]**

> Dorzagliatin, a diabetes drug approved in China in 2022, makes glucokinase (the liver and pancreas form of hexokinase) phosphorylate glucose faster. Raising the glucose concentration doesn't push the drug off the enzyme.

**Claim:** So dorzagliatin binds glucokinase's active site in place of glucose, since it changes how fast glucose is phosphorylated.

**Answer: False**

An enzyme early in an ATP-producing pathway is controlled by small molecules that bind away from its active site. Which pair of controls would best match the cell's energy needs?
